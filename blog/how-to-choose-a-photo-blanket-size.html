How to Choose a Photo Blanket Size

Published September 14, 2026 | Updated for Holiday Season 2026

Short answer: Choose the blanket size from where it will live: a 30x40 throw suits a chair and one face, a 50x60 suits a sofa and two to three people, and a 60x80 suits a bed or a wall. Bigger sizes need more resolution, so check the file at print size before ordering. For gifting, 50x60 is the safest single choice.

Bottom line: Send your highest resolution file and start with a 50x60 throw unless the blanket must cover a bed or fit a wide group shot.

Throw or Queen: Start With the Use

A throw size near 50x60 inches is the safe default: it drapes over one person on a sofa, folds for a gift, and ships in a standard box. A queen size near 60x80 inches covers a bed or wraps two people on a couch. Pick the use before the photo, because a blanket that is too big looks lost on a chair and one that is too small will not cover a bed no matter how good the print happens to be.

Wall hangings break the rules. A photo blanket pinned or rod-hung on a wall can be queen size or larger because no one has to wrap in it, and the wall hides the backing. Couch blankets should match the seat width so the print sits where people see it. If you are unsure, the throw size is the gift that almost never gets returned, because it fits every sofa and most chairs without overwhelming the room.

Think about the recipient's home before you commit. A student in a small apartment wants a throw that lives on the bed corner, while a parent with a big sectional can use a queen across the back. The custom blankets range covers both, so the size decision is yours, not the catalog's. Match the blanket to the space it will actually land in, and the photo gets seen daily.

Photo Resolution at Each Size

Resolution needs grow with size because the same photo spreads over more inches. A throw at 50x60 looks fine from a 1500 pixel image, while a queen at 60x80 wants 2500 pixels or more to stay sharp. Below those numbers the print softens, and on a queen a soft photo is obvious across the whole bed. Always check the seller's minimum before uploading a treasured shot you cannot retake.

A tightly cropped face survives upscaling better than a wide landscape with fine detail. If your only file is small, choose the throw size and crop to the key subject rather than stretching it across a queen. Our own grandma photo blanket looked best as a throw because the source was a phone snapshot, not a studio file. Match the size to the pixels you actually have, and the print stays believable.

If your photo is old or scanned, clean it before upload. A quick pass that lifts shadows and sharpens edges helps more than a bigger blanket size, because the weave shows every soft pixel. Ask a family member for the original phone file rather than the compressed version sent in a message, since those are shrunk to save data and print muddy. The best size in the world cannot fix a source that was already small. Spend ten minutes on the file and the throw or queen both come out sharper.

Group Photos and Wide Scenes

Group photos need width, so a queen or at least a 60 inch wide throw shows more faces without cropping someone out. A small throw forces you to drop people from the edges or shrink them to dots. For a family of six or a wedding party, go wide and tall so heads stay readable from across the room. The extra size is what makes the gift land, because everyone can find themselves in it.

For a wedding or reunion, mock up the crop before you order. Lay the group in a row and picture the blanket edge cutting through the ends; if two people would lose a shoulder, go queen and keep everyone whole. A tight throw crop that chops the lineup is the gift nobody wants to display. Width is your friend with crowds, so resist the square throw for anything over four faces and let the scene breathe across the larger size.

  • Use a 60 inch wide or queen size for groups over four people
  • Crop to include everyone before uploading, not after printing
  • Keep faces large by choosing a taller size for standing groups
  • Send the original file, not a compressed message attachment
  • Preview the layout and move subjects away from the seams
  • Pick a throw size only for couples or single-subject photos

Wall Versus Couch

A blanket hung on a wall can be larger and busier because it is viewed from a distance and never folded. Queen and even larger sizes work as wall art above a bed or sofa. A couch blanket is touched daily, so it should match the seat and the user, and the print should survive washing since it will be laundered. The two uses want different sizes and different care, so decide the location before ordering.

If the blanket lives on a wall, edge-to-edge printing looks best and a sherpa backing adds depth. If it lives on the couch, a softer fleece front feels better to the skin and washes easier. Decide the location before ordering, because a queen wall blanket and a throw couch blanket are different products despite the same photo. Our pet memorial blanket guide covers soft-front options for daily cuddling, and the same logic helps here.

Gift Wrapping and Shipping

Size drives the box. A throw folds into a standard shipping carton and wraps in a normal gift bag, while a queen needs a large flat mailer and creative wrapping. If you are mailing the gift, the throw is cheaper and less likely to crease in transit. A queen that arrives folded in a tight box can show a center line until washed, so plan the unboxing and tell the recipient to launder before first display.

For presentation, a throw rolls into a ribbon-tied tube that looks intentional, while a queen is better given in a fabric tote. Either way, tell the recipient it is a photo blanket so they unfold it the right way. See our notes on washing printed blankets for the first-wash that relaxes any fold line. A wrinkled queen crammed in a small box is the most common first-impression complaint we hear from buyers.

Shipping cost follows size too. A throw ships at standard parcel rates and often qualifies for the cheaper tiers, while a queen may need oversized pricing that surprises the sender. If you are mailing across the country, the throw saves real money and arrives with fewer creases. A queen is worth it when the bed or the group demands it, but for a simple thoughtful gift the throw is lighter to post and lighter on the wallet, with no loss of sentiment in the print.

Size Quick Reference

When the photo and the use point different ways, let the use win and crop the photo to fit. The table below lines up the common photo blanket sizes with their real dimensions and the buyer who should order each, so you can decide in one read instead of guessing from a product thumbnail that never shows scale. Keep the throw as your default and reach for queen only when the bed or the group demands it.

One last tip: order the size you will actually use, not the size that looks impressive in the cart. A queen that never leaves the linen closet is a wasted photo, while a throw on the back of the sofa gets seen every day. Sentiment is in the use, not the inches. Pick the throw for most gifts, step up to queen only for a bed or a wide group, and the blanket will earn its place instead of collecting dust.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size photo blanket is best for a gift?

A 50x60 inch throw is the best gift size for most people. It drapes over one person on a sofa, folds into a standard box, and rarely gets returned. Choose a queen near 60x80 inches only when the blanket must cover a bed or show a wide group photo. Match the size to the use first, then crop the image to fit the blanket you pick. The throw is the safe, almost universal choice for a first photo blanket.

How many pixels does a photo blanket need?

A throw around 50x60 inches looks good from about 1500 pixels on the long edge, while a queen near 60x80 wants 2500 pixels or more to stay sharp. Below those, the print softens and a queen shows it across the whole bed. Send the original file rather than a compressed message attachment, and crop to the subject instead of stretching a small photo. Resolution is the quiet factor behind a crisp finished blanket.

Can a photo blanket hang on a wall?

Yes, and wall hangings can be larger because no one wraps in them and the wall hides the backing. Queen size or bigger works above a bed or sofa as art. Use edge-to-edge printing and a sherpa backing for depth. A wall blanket is a display piece, so pick size for the wall space, not for a person, and skip daily washing. The blanket becomes decor, so go bold with the dimensions and the image.

Is a queen photo blanket too big to gift?

It can be for one person, because a queen near 60x80 inches is built to cover a bed, not to drape on a chair. It ships in a large mailer and needs creative wrapping. For a single recipient, the throw size is the safer, cheaper gift. Choose queen when the recipient wants bed coverage or a wide family group shot that a throw would crop. Size to the person, then to the photo.

How do I wrap a large photo blanket?

A throw rolls into a ribbon-tied tube that looks planned, and fits a standard gift bag. A queen is better given in a fabric tote or large flat mailer because it creases if crammed in a small box. Tell the recipient it is a photo blanket so they unfold it correctly. Wash before first use to relax any fold line from shipping, and the print will look as intended on the bed or wall.
