Custom Pillow Buying Guide 2026

Published September 14, 2026 | Updated for Holiday Season 2026

Short answer: A custom pillow is a printed cover plus a separate insert, and the insert decides how the pillow feels and sits. Covers from $19.99 suit people who already own inserts; bundles from $34.99 are the simplest start. Match the cover to the insert size, choose polyester fill for daily use, and keep one spare cover per insert for seasonal swaps.

Bottom line: Order one 18x18 cover-and-insert set first to learn the seller's color and fabric, then scale up once you trust the print.

Cover Versus All-in-One Insert

A custom pillow usually arrives as either a printed cover alone or a cover permanently sealed around a fill. The cover-plus-insert route lets you swap the insert when it flattens and wash the cover without wrecking the shape. The all-in-one style is cheaper and lighter to ship, but once the fill clumps you replace the whole thing. For a gift that gets daily use, the two-piece build pays for itself by year two.

Most sellers, including our page for custom pillows, sell covers and inserts separately so you can mix a poly-filled insert with a cotton cover. If you already own good inserts at home, buying covers only is the cheapest way to refresh a room. Check the zip type before ordering, because a hidden zipper keeps the print clean on the front face and a visible one can press a line into the design.

Think about who receives the pillow before you choose the build. A student in a dorm wants the light all-in-one that survives a move, while a parent upgrading a living room wants covers they can rotate and wash. The insert market is standard, so a good cover outlives several fills. Spend the money on the cover print and the zip, and treat the insert as a replaceable part.

Picking the Right Size

Square 16x16 and 18x18 inch pillows suit most sofas and reading chairs, while 20x20 and 22x22 inch sizes read as bolder accent pieces and need larger inserts to look full. A 12x20 inch lumbar shape supports the lower back and works on beds and office chairs. Measure the cushion you want to dress and order the cover a half inch smaller than the insert so the pillow stays plump rather than saggy.

Size also changes how much of your photo shows. A small 16x16 cover crops group shots to a few faces, while an 18x18 or 20x20 cover keeps more of the scene. If the image matters more than the cushion, go one size up rather than down, since a slightly larger print almost always looks better than a tight crop. For a first order, 18x18 is the size that fits the most spaces without overwhelming them.

Square versus rectangular also matters for the room. A 12x20 lumbar pillow breaks up a row of squares and supports the lower back on a sofa or bed, while a 16x20 rectangle reads as a bench or window-seat pillow. If you are dressing a pair of chairs, two 18x18 covers with a 20x20 insert on each look balanced and full. Avoid mixing too many sizes on one sofa, because the eye reads mismatch as mistake. Pick one hero size and repeat it, then add one smaller accent if the space allows the contrast.

Print Quality and Fabric Weave

Polyester woven fabric takes dye-sublimation ink with bright, edge-to-edge color that survives washing better than cotton. Cotton and linen blends feel nicer against the skin and photograph as softer, more muted tones, but fine text and small faces lose sharpness. A tight weave shows detail; a loose weave blurs it. Ask for a proof or preview before committing a treasured photo to a permanent print.

Edge-to-edge printing means the design reaches the seams, while a bordered print leaves a plain margin. Bordered prints hide small misregistration and look tidy on a shelf, but edge-to-edge feels more custom. Whichever you choose, send the highest resolution file you have, because the weave can only show what the source image contains. A phone snapshot works for a throw pillow, yet a studio file shines on a large square cover.

Color accuracy is the other half of print quality. Sublimation on polyester leans slightly bright and saturated, which most people like for photos, while cotton prints read more natural and a touch dull. If your image is mostly soft pastels, cotton may suit it better; if it is a bold graphic or a vivid family photo, polyester keeps the punch. Match the fabric to the art, not just to the budget you had in mind.

Fill Types and How They Feel

Polyester fiberfill is the default: light, washable, and cheap, though it flattens faster than you expect. A microfiber or down-alternative insert holds loft longer and feels closer to a hotel pillow. Foam inserts keep a firm shape for lumbar and floor cushions but weigh more and cost more. Match the fill to how the pillow is used, not to the cover print, because the inside decides comfort more than the outside.

A good rule is to buy the insert one size larger than the cover so the corners stay squared. A 20x20 cover over an 18x18 insert looks sad and saggy, while an 18x18 cover over a 20x20 insert strains the zip. The fill does more for comfort than the print ever will, so do not skip it to save a few dollars. A firm insert also hides a thin cover and makes the whole piece feel more expensive than it was.

Foam and buckwheat fills serve niche needs. Foam holds a firm, flat shape for floor cushions and reading nooks where a soft pillow would collapse, while buckwheat stays cool and molds to the body but rustles and weighs a lot. Neither suits a printed photo cover meant for the sofa, where polyester or down-alternative wins on look and comfort. Reserve specialty fills for function pieces and keep the decorative printed pillows on standard soft inserts that photograph as plump and inviting.

Care That Keeps the Print Bright

Wash custom pillow covers inside out on cold, gentle cycle, and line dry or tumble on low. Heat and harsh detergent are what fade a printed weave, not the act of washing itself. Remove the insert first so the cover dries faster and the fill stays dry. Spot clean small marks instead of a full wash whenever you can, because every cycle costs a little print life. Our guide to washing printed blankets uses the same cold-and-gentle logic for printed fabric.

One more habit protects the print more than any product: rotate your covers. If you keep three printed covers on two inserts, you wash each cover a third as often and the designs stay bright for years. A spare insert costs less than a replacement cover, and the rotation also hides wear at the zip. Treat covers like a small wardrobe for the room, and the pillow you gifted will look new long after the occasion that prompted it has passed.

  • Turn the cover inside out and close the zip before washing
  • Wash cold on gentle with mild liquid detergent, no softener
  • Air dry flat or tumble on low, never on high heat
  • Remove the insert before washing so the fill stays dry
  • Spot clean small marks instead of running a full wash
  • Store spare covers folded in a cool, dry place away from sun

Price Tiers and What You Get

You can spend very little or quite a lot on a custom pillow, and the gap shows up in fabric, fill, and print method rather than in the size. Budget covers print fine for decorative use but use thinner weave and lighter fill. Mid and premium tiers add denser fabric, better zippers, and inserts that hold shape. The table below maps common options to the buyer they suit best, so you can match the tier to the use.

Price also tracks the insert. A cover-only order looks cheap until you add a quality insert, so compare the full set when you shop. A photo pillow gift built from a mid tier reads as thoughtful without blowing the budget, while a bargain cover on a flat insert looks tired within a month. Spend on the fill and the zip, and the print will thank you over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wash a custom pillow cover in the machine?

Yes, wash the cover alone on cold, gentle cycle with mild detergent, turned inside out and unzipped. Remove the insert first so the cover dries quickly and the fill stays dry. Air drying flat keeps the print bright far longer than any tumble setting, and skipping fabric softener avoids a cloudy coating on the weave. The insert itself should not go through a normal wash.

Do custom pillows come with an insert?

It depends on the listing. Many sellers ship the printed cover only, which is cheaper to post and lets you reuse inserts you own. Others include a polyester insert sealed inside. Our custom pillow page sells covers and inserts separately so you can pair a soft cotton cover with a firmer fill of your choice. Always read the product line before you assume the insert is included in the price.

What size custom pillow should I buy for a sofa?

An 18x18 inch cover with a 20x20 insert fits most sofas and reads as a standard accent. For a bolder look use 20x20 or 22x22 covers. Match the cover to the insert one size up so corners stay square. Smaller 16x16 covers suit chairs and tightly spaced cushions where bigger squares look crowded. Measure the seat before ordering so the finished pillow sits the way you pictured it.

Will the photo look sharp on a pillow?

It will if the source image is high resolution and the weave is tight. Polyester sublimation shows the most detail and bright color, while cotton and linen soften fine lines and small faces. Send the largest file you have and ask for a preview before printing a treasured photo, because the fabric can only display what the original contains. A crisp source image is the single biggest factor in a sharp finished print.

How long does a custom pillow print last?

With cold, gentle washing and low-heat drying, a sublimated polyester print stays bright for years of normal use. Cotton prints fade a little sooner but still last well. The fastest killers are hot water, high dryer heat, and fabric softener. Treat the cover like any printed textile and the design outlasts the insert, which you can replace separately. A cared-for cover looks new long after the fill has gone flat.
